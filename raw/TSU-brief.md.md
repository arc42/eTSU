I, Gus Renoir, work out of the Head Office in New York.  We specialize in showing the works of young up-and-coming artists.  Our founder, the Creative Genius, scours the globe looking for the up-and-comings.  He sends us at the head office a tour concept.
**1.  Putting Together a Tour...
**A tour is a sequence of shows, and a show is a tour stop at one of our galleries. A tour is usually showing the work of one artist, but a group tour is put together on occasion -- usually when the artists don’t have a large enough body of work for a complete show.
	We make money by selling the pieces on tour, but not all pieces on tour are for sale. Some are on loan by the artist, or by a private owner, or a museum. Once we understand the tour concept, we contact the artist to see if we can reach an agreement on what pieces will be on tour, the commission percentage to the artist, the minimum sale price for each piece, and the minimum museum sale price for each piece. The minimum museum sale price is almost always lower than the minimum sale price to a customer, since the artists all have big egos, and will do most anything to have a piece hang in a museum’s collection.

If we can get agreement on all these terms, we then contact the owners and museums to try to reach lending agreements for those pieces they own. These lending agreements include things like the amount of insurance we will buy for the piece while on tour, the duration of the loan, the cleaning at the end of the tour by a professional cleaner/restorer.
	If this is all okay, then we go to a formal contract with the artist because we know that the tour is a “Go.” Now we arrange shipping and insurance for the entire tour from/to the “home” gallery. The “home” gallery is where the tour commences and where all the pieces will return when it concludes. Shipping and insurance is always arranged with one shipper and one insurer for the entire tour. That’s the way those specialty companies demand to work.  We now tell each TSU gallery about their dates and when to expect details and when to start on customs paperwork and local publicity.

**2. Making Money...
**We usually make commission on sales of pieces. Typically it’s 20% to 40%.  We contract with the artist for a minimum sale price, but we adjust (float) the price as the tour proceeds.  The price is fixed for each tour stop, but we may re-set it as the tour moves.  Sometimes if an artist is really “hanging out” (selling really well) we will even add new pieces for sale to the tour.  We’ve even had a few entrepreneurial artists creating new work for later tour stops while the tour is underway.

**3. Getting the Money...
**Buying art is supposed to be a genteel activity, so we have to follow tradition here. If a buyer wants to purchase a piece, the buyer is lead to a gallery manager’s back office for a glass of champagne while the paperwork is drawn up and signed. The deal is always the same. It is as follows: Once the buyer has signed intent to purchase a piece, that buyer has 24 hours to pony up 10% of the agreed on price. (If the purchase is on a Friday or Saturday, then he has until that time on Monday.) The rest of the money is due in 14 days from signing, no matter what day of the week.  If they miss the 10%, then the piece becomes available for sale again. If they miss the other 90% then the piece becomes available for sale again, and we keep half of the 10%, and return the other half to the failed buyer.  This returned money is known in our business as “The Insult.” Once we have all the money, then we tell the artist that the piece has been sold and we immediately pay the artist 1/2 of their due commission. We pay them the other half 5 days after the close of the tour. Remember, the piece still stays on tour.  We don’t let the buyer take the piece until the tour is over. Heaven forbid, but if something should happen to a piece on tour after it has been sold, then that’s why we have insurance. We refund all the money back to the buyer. The artists gets to keep the half of their commission; we eat that expense out of the insurance payment.
