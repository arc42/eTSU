# Interview notes: front-desk checkout process

**Date:** 2026-08-14
**Present:** Head Librarian; a library member (regular visitor); notes taken
by facilitator. A second staff member who covers weekend shifts was mentioned
several times but did not attend and is not named below.

## Head Librarian

We've run the front desk the same way for a decade: a member brings a book to
the counter, I look up their card, and I write the **loan** into the ledger by
hand — title, member number, due date. Returns are the same in reverse. The
ledger is the only record we have, and it lives in one physical binder, so if
two of us are on shift we're forever waiting for the binder to be free.

Honestly the biggest problem is speed. During the after-school rush we get a
queue out the door, and it should be fast — right now a single checkout takes
whatever it takes, could be thirty seconds, could be four minutes if the
ledger's already open to the wrong page or the kid's card number is smudged.

Weekends are worse. My weekend colleague does it slightly differently — she
checks the shelf card in the *book* first, not the member's card, and only
writes to the ledger afterwards. I don't love that order because if she gets
interrupted mid-checkout, the book looks like it's already out even though
nothing's logged yet. I've asked her to switch but it hasn't stuck.

Another thing: reservations. If a book is out and a member wants it next, we
're supposed to write their name on a card and tuck it inside the front cover.
Half the time that card goes missing before the book comes back.

## Member (regular visitor)

I come in most weeks. Whoever's at the desk when I check out is different
every time and it shows — one of them wants my card first, the other wants
the book first, and if I hand over the wrong thing first they get flustered.
Can you just pick one way?

The other thing — and I've said this before — the checkout itself is fine
once it starts, it's the **waiting** that's the problem. I timed it once on a
Tuesday afternoon: eleven minutes in line for what the librarian tells me is
a "thirty-second job." That doesn't add up to me. Either the job isn't
thirty seconds or something's going wrong before it even starts.

I also don't think it's actually true that reservations get lost "half the
time" — I've put a hold on a book twice this year and got both of them. Maybe
it's more about *which* staff member is on.

## Facilitator notes (raw, unedited)

- Two vocabularies for the same record: Head Librarian says "the loan," the
  member never uses that word at all and just says "checking out" / "the
  checkout." Same event, two names — worth a glossary pass.
- Unquantified quality wish: "it should be fast" (Head Librarian) has no
  number attached. The member's timed data point (11 minutes) is the closest
  thing to a measurement in the room and it wasn't solicited — it surfaced on
  its own.
- Outright contradiction: Head Librarian says reservation cards go missing
  "half the time"; the member reports 2 for 2 this year. Both may be true for
  different staff members, which is itself a finding, not a resolution.
- Unnamed stakeholder: the weekend colleague is discussed at length (different
  checkout order, a source of process drift) but never named in this
  transcript. Worth a stakeholder page once someone gets her name.
